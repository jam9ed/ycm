Theme & identity

Desert Tan
& Hull Navy

The colours come off the boats, not out of a nautical clip-art set. A warm paper ground rather than clinical white, deep hull navy for ink, hairline rules instead of drop shadows, and one accent held back for money. No ropes, no portholes, no anchors — the current site has roughly two hundred ⚓️ emoji doing the job of a horizontal rule.

Signal is for price drops and nothing else. Brass marks featured stock. Green means a boat you can buy today. If everything is accented, nothing is — that is the failure mode of the current site's red-and-blue everywhere.

Wordmark & hull rule Recommended

The hull silhouette does double duty as the rule between the two lines. It is one idea, executed once, and it is the only option here that stays clean when it shrinks. Sets in the site's own type, so there is no font to license or outline.

Whaler bow-on Use as the icon

From Dave's own sketch: a straight deck line, topsides flaring out to meet it, and the cathedral bottom below — two tunnels humping up either side of a sharp centre keel — with the console on deck. Earlier attempts of mine arched the sheer and rounded everything off; this is tighter, more specific to the boat, and holds down to 15px once filled rather than stroked.

YCM

YCM monogram Keep in reserve

Your top-right option, cleaned up. The hull-as-underline is a good move and this is the closest of the generated four to a modern mark. But "YCM" means nothing to a first-time visitor arriving from a Facebook post — it is a good secondary mark for hats and hull stickers, not the thing at the top of the page.

Heritage badge Not this

Your roundel and shield options, reduced to their best case — and they still lose. A badge needs detail to look like a badge, detail is the first thing that dies at small sizes, and every marine dealer in America already has one. It also fights the word "minimalist" directly.

Also: both generated badges say 1978. Nothing in the archived site supports that date — the footer reads ©2020 and the copy describes restructuring at the start of Covid. Do not set a founding year in metal until you are sure of it.

The recommended lockup, at the sizes that decide it

The rubrail and the wake drop out below about 24px, leaving the tri-hull section and the waterline. That is deliberate: a mark needing all of its parts at every size is a mark that renders as mush in a browser tab.

One grotesque throughout, set tight, with tabular numerals so prices and hours line up in a column. No display face to license, no web font to wait for, nothing to go wrong on a slow connection — which is the whole argument, given the current site loads twenty-six font files.

One thing to decide before any of this gets made: the tagline. “A different kind of boat dealer” is doing real work on the current site and appears in almost every post — but it describes the operation, not the boats. If it stays, it belongs under the wordmark in the small caps you see above, not inside the mark.
